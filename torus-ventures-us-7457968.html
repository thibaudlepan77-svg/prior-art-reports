Torus Ventures and US 7,457,968, prior art for defendants

Two IETF standards from June 1999, not cited on the patent, charted against claim 1 of US 7,457,968 under the plaintiff's own reading of it.

The patent

US 7,457,968 B2, "method and system for a recursive security protocol for digital copyright control", a continuation of US 7,203,844. Earliest priority date 20 June 2002.

Torus Ventures LLC sued eleven companies in the Western District of Texas on 4 September 2026, and the nine complaints I could read assert this patent. Their claim charts map claim 1 onto TLS 1.3 on the defendant's website, reading the decryption algorithm as the cipher suite named in a record and the second algorithm as the signature algorithm inside the decrypted handshake.

What the search found

  • A 1999 standards-track specification in which a message can be signed and then encrypted, each layer naming its own algorithm in the data, the structure the complaints read claim 1 onto.
  • Its 1999 companion for e-mail, which states that signed and encrypted formats may be nested and that signing may come first.

Where it is weak. The first chart rests on the plaintiff's reading. Under a strict reading the second chart, one encrypted envelope nested in another, carries the argument alone, and it uses an algorithm identifier rather than the algorithm's code. Claims 3 to 19 are mostly not covered.
